How I work

What "principal" means to me, day to day

Numbers every two weeks

A bi-weekly adoption report from our analytics. Decisions come from usage, not from the loudest opinion.

Productize, don't customize

One-off client requests become features for every client. Two options go to clients before we build.

The smallest change that works

Hidden customization needed a clearer entry point, not a new editor that would confuse current users.

Systems the team reuses

Design system, one documentation standard, a tone-of-voice guide developers read too.

Close to engineering

I test what ships, and I wrote the PR template that makes features testable for PMs and designers.

Research that ends in a decision

Benchmarks, personas, feature matrices. Each one ends with a choice the team can act on.

Designing with AI

AI does the checking.
I do the deciding.

AI changed where my time goes, not what the job is. I write reusable Claude skills that encode how our team works, and connect them to Figma and to our analytics. The repetitive reviews run in minutes, and I spend the time on what to build, what to cut and when something is good enough to ship.

  • Skill

    UX writing review

    Reads the copy of a Figma flow, checks it against our tone of voice and flags placeholder text before it reaches production.

  • Skill

    Flow review

    Checks that a flow covers its states (empty, error, limits) and stays consistent with the documented design system.

  • Skill

    Component docs

    Generates the standard documentation frame for a component directly in Figma, so the design system stays documented as it grows.

  • Workflow

    Analytics report

    Claude pulls the numbers from PostHog and drafts the bi-weekly report; I read it, question it and write the recommendations.

Side project

Made with malafede

As a kid I always had my hands in something: dough, glue, cardboard, whatever was on the table. I was the one who built things.

That girl is why I studied industrial design. Then my work moved to screens, and I love it, but I missed making something I could hold.

Malafede is where she lives now. The yarn comes from fashion's leftovers, fabric scraps that would have been thrown away. I crochet every bag by hand, one at a time, and each one gets a name.
